What this is

Product managers are the closest role in the company to strategy work. Almost none are trained for it.

You already own the trade-offs. You already say no. What you often can’t do — in the room, under pressure, in front of an executive who has a different idea — is show the reasoning as a chain of cause and effect that connects a sprint to a company objective.

This is the product edition of Intent, Value & Impact: the same two-day method, rebuilt end to end around the product role. You apply Balanced Scorecard methodology to your product specifically, build a product strategy map, define metrics that go past feature velocity, and leave with a roadmap you can defend on evidence rather than conviction.

No prior Balanced Scorecard experience required. Bring a real product — you’ll be working on it the whole time.

Who it’s for

For product people who want the roadmap taken seriously.

This course is for you if…

  • You’re a product manager, product owner or product lead
  • You want to move from feature delivery to strategic product ownership
  • You’re an IC PM, senior/principal PM, group PM or head of product
  • Your roadmap gets read as a wish list rather than a strategy
  • You’re tired of defending priorities with opinion instead of logic

Look elsewhere if…

  • You’re not in a product role — take the general Intent, Value & Impact edition
  • You want a discovery or user-research course; this is about strategic connection
  • You want organizational strategy — that’s the VAST five-day

What you leave with

Seven shifts, applied to your actual product.

Everything here gets built on a real product over the two days.

  • Clearer strategic alignment — your roadmap visibly supports organizational goals, not just the loudest user requests
  • Stronger measurement — output metrics (features shipped) replaced by outcome metrics that show real product impact
  • Better cross-functional collaboration — engineering, design and sales get a shared language for why the roadmap looks the way it does
  • More confident prioritization — cause-and-effect thinking you can use to defend roadmap trade-offs
  • Increased visibility and influence — the product’s strategic contribution shown to leadership, not just a release log
  • Accountability and learning — sprint-level work tied to long-term product value creation
  • Greater organizational impact — a visibly connected product strategy lifts the whole organization’s performance

The agenda

Eight sessions across two days.

Day 1 goes from your value proposition to a balanced set of product objectives. Day 2 turns those into a map, metrics, a sequenced roadmap and a plan you sign.

DAY 1 You Must Add Value → Defining ImpactSessions 1–4 · from feature factory to strategic product partner ›
Morning — sessions 1 & 2
  • You Must Add Value — self-assessment: what share of your week is strategic impact versus backlog management?
  • The value hierarchy: Backlog Execution → Product Expertise → Roadmap Enablement → Strategic Partnership
  • Personal value proposition exercise: where does your product actually create value, and who feels what you ship?
  • Setting Goals & Intent — the four perspectives applied to your product; product strategy as a hypothesis, not a fixed roadmap
  • Strategic context analysis and the strategic intent workshop for your product
Afternoon — sessions 3 & 4
  • You Are Here — product maturity assessment across key capability areas
  • Stakeholder value assessment: how is your product’s value seen by execs, customers and engineering — and where do those diverge?
  • SWOT with a strategic lens, and a current-state synthesis
  • Themes & Objectives — product theme generation, evaluation and prioritization
  • Objectives across Financial (revenue, margin, retention), Customer, Process (discovery and development) and Capability & Capacity
You leave the day with: an articulated product value proposition, a strategic intent statement for the product, a current-state assessment, and 3–5 product themes carrying 10–14 balanced objectives.
DAY 2 Telling the Value Story → Make It HappenSessions 5–8 · product strategy map, metrics past velocity, roadmap, 90-day plan ›
Morning — sessions 5 & 6
  • Telling the Value Story — product strategy map construction and cause-and-effect linkage
  • Roadmap narrative development, and stakeholder-specific messaging that keeps the core story intact
  • Value dialogue simulation: executive role-play and handling roadmap pushback
  • Measure What Matters — output versus outcome, leading versus lagging, vanity metrics versus value metrics
  • KPI development by objective; OKR integration and cascading; measurement system design
Afternoon — sessions 7 & 8
  • Prioritize & Deliver — initiative identification and initiative-to-objective mapping
  • Prioritization: impact versus effort (RICE/ICE-style) and strategic alignment scoring
  • Roadmap sequencing, dependency mapping, MVP and incremental value planning
  • Make It Happen — impediment assessment and root cause analysis
  • 30/60/90-day roadmap commitment, accountability framework, and public commitment
You leave the day with: a visual product strategy map, a balanced metric set beyond velocity, a prioritized and sequenced initiative roadmap, and a signed 90-day plan.

How it runs

Delivered in the room, or live online.

In person

A facilitated working session — breakout space, wall work, and cross-functional groups. This is the format the material was designed for.

Live online

Same facilitator, same exercises, run in a virtual workspace. Fully live — not a recorded course you watch alone.

Private cohort

Run for your organization, on your strategy, with your own examples. Run it for a whole product org and every PM’s map connects to the same objectives.

  • Digital materials, always. Workbook, templates, and worksheets go out digitally — in person or online. Nobody wants to carry a binder home.
  • Interactive by design. Expect individual reflection and small-group exercises throughout, not a lecture with slides.
  • Every participant leaves with their own artifacts — the deliverables listed in the agenda above are yours, built on your real work.
